Formula

The calculation used by this tool is:

ROI = ((Annual Profit + Annual Savings − Investment) ÷ Investment) × 100

The calculation compares the annual financial benefits generated by a manufacturing investment with the investment required to implement it.

Example

A $100,000 manufacturing investment produces $30,000 in annual additional profit and $20,000 in annual savings.

Annual benefit: $30,000 + $20,000 = $50,000

Manufacturing ROI: (($50,000 − $100,000) ÷ $100,000) × 100 = −50%

Under this simple first-year ROI model, the manufacturing investment produces a −50% ROI because its first-year benefits do not fully recover the initial investment.

Actual performance can differ because of taxes, financing, depreciation, working capital, maintenance, attribution and other factors not included in a simple ROI model.

Frequently asked questions

What is ROI?

ROI, or return on investment, measures the return generated relative to the cost or investment required.

How is this calculator calculated?

Manufacturing ROI is calculated by adding annual additional profit and annual operating savings, subtracting the manufacturing investment, dividing by the investment, and multiplying by 100.

Can ROI be negative?

Yes. A negative ROI means the annual financial benefits do not recover the manufacturing investment under the assumptions used.

What should I include?

Include the initial manufacturing investment and realistic annual additional profit and operating savings attributable to the investment.

Is ROI the same as profit?

No. Profit is an absolute monetary amount, while ROI expresses the return relative to the investment.
