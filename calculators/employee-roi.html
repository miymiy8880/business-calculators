Formula

The calculation used by this tool is:

Employee ROI = ((Value Generated − Employee Cost) ÷ Employee Cost) × 100

Employee ROI expresses the measurable return generated by an employee relative to the total cost of employing that person.

Example

An employee costs $50,000 and generates $90,000 in measurable value.

Net value: $90,000 − $50,000 = $40,000

Employee ROI: ($40,000 ÷ $50,000) × 100 = 80%

Therefore, the employee generates an estimated ROI of 80% under these assumptions.

Actual performance can differ because of taxes, financing, depreciation, working capital, maintenance, attribution and other factors not included in a simple ROI model.

Frequently asked questions

What is ROI?

ROI, or return on investment, measures the return generated relative to the cost of an investment.

How is employee ROI calculated?

Employee ROI is calculated by subtracting total employee cost from measurable value generated, dividing the result by employee cost, and multiplying by 100.

Can employee ROI be negative?

Yes. A negative employee ROI means the measurable value generated is lower than the employee cost under the assumptions used.

What should I include in employee cost?

Employee cost can include salary, employer taxes, benefits, bonuses, recruitment costs, training costs and other directly attributable employment expenses.

Is employee ROI the same as profit?

No. Profit is an absolute monetary amount. ROI expresses the return relative to the cost used to generate it.
