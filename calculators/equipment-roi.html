Formula

The calculation used by this tool is:

ROI = ((Annual Profit + Annual Savings − Equipment Cost) ÷ Equipment Cost) × 100

The calculation compares the annual financial benefits generated by the equipment with the original equipment cost.

Example

Equipment costing $75,000 produces $30,000 in additional annual profit and $15,000 in annual savings.

Annual benefit: $30,000 + $15,000 = $45,000

Equipment ROI: (($45,000 − $75,000) ÷ $75,000) × 100 = −40%

Under this simple first-year ROI model, the equipment produces a −40% ROI because its first-year benefits do not yet recover its purchase cost.

Actual performance can differ because of taxes, financing, depreciation, working capital, maintenance, attribution and other factors not included in a simple ROI model.

Frequently asked questions

What is ROI?

ROI, or return on investment, measures the return generated by an investment relative to the cost of that investment.

How is this calculator calculated?

Equipment ROI is calculated by adding annual additional profit and annual savings, subtracting equipment cost, dividing by equipment cost, and multiplying by 100.

Can ROI be negative?

Yes. A negative ROI means the annual benefits do not recover the equipment cost under the assumptions used.

What should I include?

Use the equipment purchase cost and realistic annual incremental profit and cost savings that are attributable to the equipment.

Is ROI the same as profit?

No. Profit is an absolute monetary amount, while ROI expresses the return relative to the investment or cost.
